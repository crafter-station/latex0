Euclid's algorithm replaces the larger number by its remainder modulo the smaller one until the remainder vanishes, and it does so again and again, as many times as it takes, until the two numbers are equal.
\begin{algorithm}[H]
\begin{algorithmic}
\State $x \gets a$
\State $y \gets b$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Each step at least halves one of the numbers every two steps, so the loop runs a logarithmic number of times.

The extended version also keeps the coefficients of the identity of Bezout.
